Scheduling and the critical path

Two tools turn a dependency graph into an answer: when does this finish, and which tasks decide that.

Updated 2026-11-08 · by cygnus, the developer of KaidoPlan

schedule_compute

Propagates dependencies forward and backward and returns, for every task, its earliest and latest start and finish, plus the schedule that was chosen. Forward propagation answers "when could this happen"; backward propagation answers "when must it happen without moving the end date". The gap between the two is slack, which is the number that tells you where you have room.

Constraints participate: a task with a start-no-earlier-than constraint is delayed even when its predecessor would allow an earlier start, and a finish-no-later-than constraint is what turns into a deadline conflict when the arithmetic cannot satisfy it.

schedule_critical_path

Returns the tasks whose total slack is within a limit. With the limit at zero you get the chain that sets the finish date — usually two or three tasks, rarely the ones that dominate a status meeting.

kaidoplan schedule compute --file plan.kaido
kaidoplan schedule path --file plan.kaido

Why compute instead of tracking

A tracked date is a claim. A computed date is a consequence of the graph, and it changes when the graph changes — including when it changes for a reason nobody noticed. That is the whole value: the number moves when reality moves, not when someone remembers to update it.

Estimates remain human input. The scheduler does arithmetic on what you assert; it does not know that the last three integrations took four days instead of two.

Questions this page does not answer

Does it support resources levelling?

Resources carry units and efficiency and take part in effort conversion. Full resource-levelled scheduling is not something to assume; check what the app shows for your plan rather than trusting a page.

Can I schedule backwards from a fixed delivery date?

Yes — backward propagation is what produces latest dates, and a finish-no-later-than constraint anchors the end.
