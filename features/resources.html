Resources

A plan that says three days is making an assumption about who is doing the work. Resources make the assumption explicit.

Updated 2026-11-08 · by cygnus, the developer of KaidoPlan

The tools

ToolDoes
resource_addAdds a person, piece of equipment or material. units and efficiency take part in effort conversion.
resource_listLists the project's resources.

Why bother on a solo project

Because effort and duration are not the same number. Eight hours of work is one day for one person at full attention, two days at half, and four days if the "person" is also doing support that week. Recording the resource is how the plan carries that assumption instead of hiding it in an estimate.

On an AI-assisted project the resource model also covers the non-human parts: the CI runner that can only do one build at a time, the device that has to be free for a test. Those constraints behave exactly like a busy person.

What it does not do

Do not read this as full resource-levelled scheduling with automatic reallocation. Resources inform effort conversion and appear on the plan; the scheduler's job here is the dependency graph and the dates.

In practice

  • Add the people who will actually do the work, with realistic units.
  • Record shared equipment when it is a real bottleneck.
  • Re-check the critical path after a resource change: a task that becomes half-time can move the end date without any dependency changing.

Questions this page does not answer

Can I assign a task to a resource?

Tasks carry an assignee, which is the owner. Resources are the pool the plan describes; the two are related but not the same field.

Does efficiency affect dates?

It takes part in effort conversion, which is how duration is derived from effort — so yes, indirectly.
