KaidoPlan Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

KaidoPlan ("the app") keeps your project data on your own machine: the contents of your .kaido files are never uploaded, and every local feature opens, edits and saves without a licence or an account. The app talks to the outside world in exactly four ways, each spelled out below: feedback you submit yourself, the update check in the direct download, a static revocation list the direct download reads, and the licence service that the direct download contacts when you activate Pro or sign in to the optional account.

What stays on your Mac

  • Project data: the .kaido files you open and save live only where you put them. The app never uploads, backs up or syncs project content.
  • Recent projects: to reopen your last project on launch, the app creates a security-scoped bookmark for files or folders you explicitly chose, and keeps it in local preferences. Clearing "Recent projects" or removing an authorized folder deletes it.
  • MCP service (off by default): when you enable it under Project ▸ MCP Service…, the app listens on 127.0.0.1 so a locally running AI client can read and write the same plan.
    • loopback only — never the LAN, never the internet;
    • every request must carry a bearer token generated locally and shown in that panel;
    • only folders you authorized in that panel are reachable; anything else is refused;
    • turning the switch off or resetting the token cuts access immediately.

Licence and optional account (direct download only)

  • When it connects: only after you install license.json does the app send its first activation request to the licence service. After that it checks every 12 hours and only renews when the credential has less than 7 days left. No licence and no sign-in means the licence service is never contacted (the update check and the revocation list are two other things, see below, and neither carries any identifier).
  • The account is optional: activation itself does not need an account. The only thing an account gives you is a page in the browser where you can see your licences, manage (unbind) devices and delete the account.

  • How the device hash is made: the app reports a local machine identifier (the system UUID, or a random value kept in the keychain when that is unavailable), and the server hashes it once more with its own salt before storing. Nothing in the database lines up directly with a machine, and it cannot be turned back into a device identifier.
  • Not collected: phone numbers, real-name verification, raw device fingerprints, plaintext IP addresses, page content, usage behaviour, and the contents or paths of .kaido files.
  • IP addresses: used only as a hash that rotates daily for rate limiting, deleted with the rate-limit record within 48 hours; the same address cannot be followed across days.

Deleting things, and what stays

  • Deleting the account: press "Delete account" on the account page (served by the licence service, opened in a browser). It takes effect immediately — every session for that account stops working and the old sign-in state cannot be used on any endpoint; once the account has been deleted for 30 days, the daily cleanup replaces the email address with a placeholder (those 30 days exist so a mistaken deletion can still be recovered through support).
  • Unbinding a device: the same page unbinds devices one at a time and frees the seat at once. The credential on the unbound machine keeps working for at most 30 days (that window is there so you can work offline), after which the machine has to activate again.
  • Signing out: ends this one session only, deletes nothing.
  • Retention: order and licence records are kept for 10 years after the account is deleted, because tax and refund disputes need the original transaction; deleting the account does not shorten that. Everything else expires on schedule: sessions when they expire (≤30 days), sign-in codes after 10 minutes, rate-limit records after 48 hours, landing-page counters after 90 days, and the operational audit log after 400 days.

The Mac App Store build has no account and no activation

The build installed from the Mac App Store contains no code for accounts, activation or the revocation list (removed at compile time); entitlement comes from the App Store transaction. It does not contact the licence service and has no self-update check (updates go through the App Store).

Feedback you submit

  • Trigger: only when you press Submit in the Help ▸ Report a Problem… panel; until then what you typed stays on your Mac. What is sent: your description, machine details (version, build number, distribution channel, macOS version, Mac model identifier, UI language, licence type), and — only if you tick the box for them — logs and crash reports, redacted before packaging (paths keep only their last two components). The feedback is posted to this project's public issue tracker, where anyone can read and search it and where it may be indexed by search engines and third-party archives; submitting with your own GitHub account shows that account name, while having the maintainer's account (GitHub cygnusyang) post it for you does not show your account details (the body is exactly what you wrote). You cannot delete it yourself — to have it removed, leave a comment on the original issue.
  • What feedback never contains: the contents of your .kaido project files, project file paths, recent project bookmarks, the contents of your licence file, and your email address (redacted from logs before packaging).

Update check and revocation list (direct download only)

  • A few seconds after launch the app fetches update-manifest.json from GitHub Releases to see whether a newer version exists (and every 4 hours after that). That is an ordinary static download carrying no account, licence ID or device identifier.
  • Every 7 days it fetches the static revoked.json list to check whether a licence was revoked after a refund.
  • Both are served by GitHub. As with any HTTP request, the host sees your IP address; that part is governed by GitHub's own privacy policy.

This website

  • The landing pages set no cookies, carry no third-party advertising or tracking SDKs, and do no cross-session user identification.
  • Counters record only the event name (view, download click, buy click), referrer, page path, language, country and time, kept for 90 days; rate limiting uses a hash of your IP address that rotates daily, kept for 48 hours.
  • The subscribe form takes your email address and an optional tool choice when you submit it, and uses them for release announcements. To be removed from that list, email and ask — there is no self-service switch.
