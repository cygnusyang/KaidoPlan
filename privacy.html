KaidoPlan Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 November 2026

KaidoPlan ("the app") does not collect your personal data and does not send anything anywhere in the background. The only outbound transfer is feedback you submit yourself: nothing is sent until you write something under Help ▸ Report a Problem… and press Submit, and what you chose to send becomes publicly visible in an issue in the public repository.

  • No data collection: no accounts, no analytics, no background crash reporting, no advertising, no third-party SDKs. The app never uploads anything on its own.
  • Feedback you submit (the only outbound transfer): sent only when you press Submit in the Help ▸ Report a Problem… panel; until then what you typed stays on your Mac. What is sent: your description, machine details (version, build number, distribution channel, macOS version, Mac model identifier, UI language, license type), and — only if you tick the box for them — logs and crash reports, redacted before packaging (paths keep only their last two components). The feedback is posted to this project's public issue tracker, where anyone can read and search it and where it may be indexed by search engines and third-party archives; submitting with your own GitHub account shows that account name, while having the maintainer's account (GitHub cygnusyang) post it for you does not show your account details (the body is exactly what you wrote). You cannot delete it yourself — to have it removed, leave a comment on the original issue. Never uploaded: the contents of your .kaido project files, project file paths, recent project bookmarks, the contents of your license file, and your email address.
  • Project data stays local: the .kaido files you open and save live only where you put them. The app never uploads, backs up or syncs project content.
  • Recent projects: to reopen your last project on launch, the app creates a security-scoped bookmark for files or folders you explicitly chose, and keeps it in local preferences. Clearing "Recent projects" or removing an authorized folder deletes it.
  • MCP service (off by default): when you enable it under Project ▸ MCP Service…, the app listens on 127.0.0.1 so a locally running AI client can read and write the same plan.
    • loopback only — never the LAN, never the internet;
    • every request must carry a bearer token generated locally and shown in that panel;
    • only folders you authorized in that panel are reachable; anything else is refused;
    • turning the switch off or resetting the token cuts access immediately.
  • No third-party sharing: apart from feedback you submit yourself, which GitHub hosts as a public issue, the app shares nothing with any third party — there is no other data to share.

Questions: open an issue at github.com/cygnusyang/KaidoPlan.
